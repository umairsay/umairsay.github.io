FIFA World Cup ’26 — Team Identity Atlas

Every team has a shape

Four magnitude facets describe the football process each team produced: control, access, line-breaking and chance threat. Two scales describe style — attacking, from transition to possession, and defending, from deep block to front foot.

How to read it

The four magnitude facets form a single closed ribbon. Its width at each of the four stations is where that team ranked on that facet against the other 47 — a station at 90 means only about four teams did more of it — so every team ends up with a silhouette of its own.

The stations follow the attacking sequence. Control creates the platform, access gets the team into advanced spaces, line-breaking penetrates the defensive structure, and chance threat is the danger produced. Reading top to bottom follows an attack forward. Teams skip stages, though, especially in transition. Across the 48, control and access move closely together and both track chance threat, while line-breaking is close to independent of the other three. A side can dominate the ball without ever going through a defensive line, and another can break lines constantly without dominating anything.

Each style scale is one composite minus another — possession-based build-up against transition play, front-foot pressing against deep defending. That difference is then ranked across the 48, so the marker shows which way a team leaned and the number shows how far relative to everyone else.

Every facet was recalculated with each match dropped in turn. The pale halo around a ribbon, and the pale band behind each style marker, span the lowest and highest percentile a team reached across those refits. Read them as sensitivity, not statistical confidence.
